## Buying Guide: Choosing the Perfect Nursery Paint

Selecting paint for a nursery involves more than just picking a pretty color. It's a careful balance of safety, durability, and practicality. Here's what to consider:

### 1. Zero VOC (Volatile Organic Compounds)

This is non-negotiable for a nursery. VOCs are chemicals emitted as gases from certain solids or liquids, and they can have adverse short- and long-term health effects, particularly on developing respiratory systems.
*   **Look for "Zero VOC" or "No VOC":** This means the paint contains less than 5 grams per liter of VOCs. Be aware that colorants added to paint can sometimes contain VOCs, so ask if the tinting process is also zero-VOC.
*   **Low Odor:** Zero-VOC paints typically have very low odor, which is a huge plus when painting a baby's room.

### 2. Washable Finish & Durability

Nurseries are high-traffic areas prone to fingerprints, crayon marks, spit-up, and general smudges. A washable finish is crucial for maintaining a clean and fresh look.
*   **Eggshell, Satin, or Semi-Gloss:** These finishes are generally the most durable and easiest to clean.
    *   **Eggshell:** A subtle sheen, popular for walls. Good durability.
    *   **Satin:** A slightly higher sheen than eggshell, offers excellent washability and scuff resistance. Very popular for nurseries.
    *   **Semi-Gloss:** Highest sheen, very durable and easy to clean, often used for trim, but can be used on walls for a modern, highly practical look.
*   **Avoid Flat or Matte Finishes:** While beautiful, they are typically less durable and harder to clean without showing marks.

### 3. Paint and Primer in One

Many modern paints combine paint and primer. This can be a huge time-saver, especially if you're painting over a darker color or uneven surface.
*   **Benefits:** Reduces the number of coats needed, saves labor, and often provides better hide and adhesion.
*   **Considerations:** While convenient, some professional painters still prefer separate primer for optimal results, especially on very challenging surfaces. For most nursery repaints, a good paint-and-primer-in-one is sufficient.

### 4. Coverage and Application

*   **Coverage:** Look for paints with good coverage, usually measured in square feet per gallon. This dictates how much paint you'll need.
*   **Ease of Application:** Quality paints should go on smoothly, dry evenly, and resist splattering. This makes the DIY process much easier.

### 5. Price Point (Under $75 per Gallon)

You don't need to break the bank to get a safe and durable paint. Many excellent zero-VOC options are available within our target budget.
*   **Value vs. Cost:** Remember to consider the paint's longevity and ease of cleaning. A slightly more expensive, highly durable paint might save you money and effort in the long run by requiring less frequent repainting or touch-ups.

### 6. Color Choice and Mood

While not a technical feature, color plays a huge role in a nursery.
*   **Calming Hues:** Soft blues, greens, yellows, grays, and pastels are popular for creating a tranquil environment.
*   **Light Colors:** Tend to make a room feel larger and brighter, which is often desirable in a nursery.
*   **Test Swatches:** Always paint swatches on your wall and observe them at different times of day before committing to a color.
