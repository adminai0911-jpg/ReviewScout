# Best Industrial Ultrasonic Cleaner for Engine Parts and Carburetors 2024

If you are a professional mechanic or a dedicated vintage car restoration enthusiast, you know that the "old-school" method of scrubbing carburetors with wire brushes and toxic solvents is a losing battle. Carbon deposits, varnish, and stubborn grease hide in the microscopic nooks of jets and valves where tools simply can't reach.

The solution? Cavitation. Industrial ultrasonic cleaners utilize high-frequency sound waves to create millions of microscopic bubbles that implode against surfaces, stripping away grime without damaging delicate metal components. ## Buying Guide: How to Choose the Right Cleaner

Choosing the right ultrasonic cleaner isn't just about grabbing the biggest tank you can find. Consider these three critical factors:

1.  **Tank Capacity:** Measure the parts you intend to clean most often. Remember that parts must be fully submerged, so leave an extra 2–3 inches of buffer space above your parts to account for the solution level.
2.  **Heating and Timing:** Never buy a unit without a heater. Heat is essential for breaking down grease and carbonized oil. A digital timer is also necessary for repeatable results, especially if you are working with soft metals that can be etched if left in the solution for too long.
3.  **Frequency (kHz):** Most industrial cleaners operate between 28kHz and 40kHz. Lower frequencies (around 28kHz) create larger, more energetic bubbles—perfect for heavy engine grease. Higher frequencies (40kHz+) are better for delicate, precision internal components. Most all-in-one shop units use 40kHz as a versatile middle ground.
